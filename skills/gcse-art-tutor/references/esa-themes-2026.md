# 2026 ESA Themes by Exam Board

The Externally Set Assignment (ESA) paper is released to students on **2 January 2026** by all boards. Students then have approximately 10–12 weeks of preparatory work before completing a **10-hour supervised session**.

---

## AQA (8201 / 8202) — 7 Starting Points

Students choose **one** of seven starting points to develop their personal response.

### 1. Nature
- Focus on changing seasons, organic textures, the cycle of growth and decay
- Move beyond literal representation — explore macro photography, microscopic detail, abstraction of natural forms
- Consider: weather, ecosystems, the natural world in a built environment

### 2. Isolation
- Explore architectural voids, empty spaces, solitude
- Consider social and psychological dimensions: physical separation, social distancing, loneliness, introspection
- Could be represented through negative space, figure/ground relationships, absence

### 3. Movement
- Capture kinetics of the human body, animals, or temporal shifts
- Explore blur, repetition, implied motion, sequential imagery
- Consider: dance, sport, migrating animals, light trails, time-lapse thinking

### 4. Interiors
- Investigate domestic spaces, structural anatomy of rooms
- Explore perspective, unusual viewpoints, pattern within architecture, artificial lighting
- Could engage with the personal/psychological meaning of a space

### 5. Time
- Explore decay, growth, historical layering, the archive
- Consider: aging, erosion, the nature of memory and photography, fossilisation
- Could span geological time to personal memory

### 6. Magnificent
- A broad prompt referencing grandeur — expansive landscapes, sublime natural environments
- Also works at the opposite scale — the intricate magnificence of tiny creatures or objects
- Encourages awe, scale contrast, detailed rendering

### 7. Lost & Found
- Focus on remnants, rediscovery, the layering of meaning in fragments
- Objects that have been abandoned, reclaimed, or recontextualised
- Links to the Edexcel 2026 theme — students on AQA can look at Edexcel resources for additional artist inspiration

## Pearson Edexcel (1FA0 / 1AD0) — Theme: "Found"

One broad theme with **five investigative sub-themes**. Students select one sub-theme or negotiate a personal direction through their teacher.

### Found: People
- Traces of human presence, historical archives, found portraiture
- Abandoned personal items that imply a person but show absence
- Documentary photography, vernacular photography, archival images

### Found: Possessions
- Remnants of domestic life, objects charged with memory
- Discarded, inherited, or salvaged personal items
- Still life traditions reinterpreted through the "found" lens

### Found: Human-made
- Urban detritus, industrial fragments, architectural remnants
- The decay and transformation of built environments
- Found objects as sculptural or printmaking material

### Found: Environment
- Natural finds — sea glass, driftwood, bones, shells, geological samples
- Objects changed by natural processes: erosion, weathering, fossilisation
- Environmental commentary through found material

### Found: Natural World
- Biological discoveries — botanical specimens, fungi, animal remains
- Geological strata, rock formations, mineral crystals
- The scientific and aesthetic intersection of natural specimens

## OCR (J170–J176) — 5 Themes

Students choose **one** from five themes, each presented with written and visual stimuli in the paper.

### Reflections
- Visual echoes: water surfaces, mirrors, glass, polished metal
- Also inner contemplation, self-reflection, retrospection
- Challenges with inverted images, distortion, multiple viewpoints

### Connections
- Physical links: assemblage, infrastructure, wire, rope, bridges
- Invisible links: family relationships, cultural heritage, genetics, community
- Pattern and rhythm created by repeated connecting elements

### Woman
- Exploring identity, female experience, resilience, and empowerment
- Motherhood, aging, the gaze, representation of women in art history
- Can engage critically with how women have been depicted

### Layers
- Physical tiers: rock strata, collage, geological cross-section, built-up paint surface
- Psychological layers: layers of memory, identity, meaning
- Palimpsest — visible traces of what has been covered or erased

### Twisting and Turning
- Movement and dynamic form: spirals, helices, entwined and intertwined shapes
- Optical effects: op art, visual illusion, disorienting geometry
- Natural forms (DNA, shells, tornadoes) alongside architectural and textile twists

## WJEC Eduqas (C650–C656 / C651) — "Big Ideas"

The Eduqas assignment centres on overarching "Big Ideas." Current examples include:

- **Perspectives** — viewpoint, both physical and interpretive
- **Belonging** — identity, community, place, cultural roots
- **Collections** — assemblage, taxonomy, the archive, hoarding vs curating
- **Structures** — natural and manmade frameworks, architectural and biological form
- **Protest** — activism, resistance, political art, the power of images
- **Connection** — links between people, places, ideas, materials
- **Identity** — self-portrait tradition, cultural identity, persona
- **Innovation** — new technologies in art, breaking from tradition
- **Cynefin** — Welsh concept meaning "habitat" or sense of place; the land, belonging, rootedness

*Note: Eduqas releases the assignment to teachers in December for resource preparation; students are formally introduced in the spring term.*

## Choosing a Theme: Coaching Advice

Help students choose their theme using these questions:

1. **Personal connection** — "Which theme connects to something in your own life, interests, or experiences?"
2. **Artist links** — "Are there artists whose work you already love? Which themes connect to their practice?"
3. **Media fit** — "Do you have a preferred medium or technique? Which theme would work well with it?"
4. **Breadth** — "Which theme gives you the most directions to explore? Avoid themes that feel too narrow."
5. **Commitment** — "Which theme could you spend 12 weeks on without getting bored?"

**Critical warning:** Once a student commits to a theme, changing it wastes preparation time and leads to lower grades. Encourage confident commitment early.

## Preparatory Period: 12-Week Strategic Roadmap

### Weeks 1–2: Initial exploration
- Mind-map the chosen theme from multiple angles
- Gather initial primary source material: own photography (aim for at least 50 images)
- Research 2–3 initial artists connected to the theme

### Weeks 3–5: Artist studies and skill bridging
- Select 2–3 artists to study in depth
- Produce artist study pages using "skill bridging": recreate a section of their work, identify the technical gap, close the gap through experiments, apply to own work
- Begin media experimentation

### Weeks 6–8: Development and refinement
- Develop the most promising directions
- Conduct further primary observation and photography
- Refine techniques through targeted practice
- Write analytical annotations throughout

### Weeks 9–10: Final preparation
- Narrow to the final composition and approach
- Produce mock/trial runs of the final piece
- Create a detailed hour-by-hour plan for the 10-hour supervised session
- Practise working at exam pace with real media

### Weeks 11–12: Supervised 10-hour session
- Execute the final piece according to the plan
- Use preparatory work as reference — cannot be altered once exam begins
- Complete critical self-evaluation within the final hour
